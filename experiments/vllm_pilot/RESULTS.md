# vLLM generation pilot

PBS job 61178 ran vLLM 0.17.1 on one RTX A6000 using Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B-Instruct, BF16, eight held prompts sampled from the existing DAPO training pool, temperature 1, top-p 1, and a 512-token output limit. The engine was warmed up and emitted per-token sampled log probabilities. It generated 4,053 tokens in 4.13 seconds (981 tokens/s); model loading and engine setup took 36.8 seconds separately. This is generation throughput, not complete PPO update throughput.

Jobs 61179 and 61180 used the same eight prompts and limit with Hugging Face generation, microbatch 4 and generated-token score calculation, excluding model load: FP32 3,900 tokens / 19.86 s = 196 tokens/s; BF16 3,860 tokens / 20.54 s = 188 tokens/s. Different random samples and batch sizes mean these are a small workload comparison, not a controlled estimate of an end-to-end training speedup. vLLM used batch 8 while the Hugging Face benchmark reproduced the training generation microbatch 4. Warmup was excluded for both.

For the 4,053 vLLM-sampled tokens, comparing recorded sampler log probabilities to a BF16 Hugging Face full-sequence forward pass produced absolute importance-ratio deviations: median 0.00026%, p95 4.99%, p99 10.92%, maximum 35.28%, and 0.173% of tokens above 20%. These are sampled-token statistics, not full-distribution divergence. The same reward function returned the same result on a repeated call for all eight outputs; this is not independent verification of mathematical correctness.

Setup required an isolated environment (`/scratch/tsa5252/.venvs/vllm-pilot`), an updated C++ library path, converting PBS's assigned GPU UUID to its numeric identifier, and moving flashinfer caches to scratch. A background usage-statistics thread also hit the home quota; the pilot nevertheless exited successfully. The vLLM pilot does not perform PPO training, synchronize updated weights, or implement behavior-policy correction. The four existing training jobs were not modified. Artifacts: `runs/vllm_pilot/`, `pbs_vllm_pilot.sh`, and `experiments/vllm_pilot/`.

## One full MLC-PPO update

PBS job 61185 completed one 28-head MLC-PPO update using 64 DAPO problems, response cap 2,048, actor microbatch 4, two PPO epochs, actual verifier rewards and standalone/TRL reward parity checks. The vLLM policy was BF16; the training policy and optimizer were FP32. vLLM generated all rollouts from the initial checkpoint, exited, and a separate training process loaded the same checkpoint and performed the update. This is a single-update benchmark, not a persistent engine or a weight-synchronization test. The actual sampler probabilities were retained and logged; the PPO clipping denominator used detached full-sequence old-policy probabilities. No correction for sampler/trainer mismatch was applied.

The job generated 49,988 response tokens in 13.47 s, after 19.86 s of vLLM engine initialization. Including process startup and model loading, the generation process took 42.25 s, and the training process took 150.07 s, for 192.31 s total. The isolated trainer's own update timer was 141.29 s; rollout feature extraction, scoring and mismatch checks totaled 17.31 s. Peak PyTorch training allocation was 40.61 GB. All unchanged-policy PPO ratio checks passed at displayed precision; the process exited successfully.

The prior running FP32 all-layer job's first update logged 50,769 generated tokens and 538.75 s after its model initialization. Its response samples and prompt-batch padding differ, and its timer does not include startup, so the approximate 2.8x comparison to the vLLM job's 192.31 s total is indicative, not a controlled matched-trajectory benchmark. The vLLM job includes startup and reload but excludes any repeated online weight-transfer cost.

Across the 49,988 sampled tokens, 0.0480% had an absolute behavior-vs-training probability-ratio deviation above 20%; the maximum was 55.10%. Batch-level p95 absolute deviations ranged from 1.72% to 4.16%. Full-sequence old-vs-new ratio checks do not eliminate or correct this behavior mismatch. No long-running comparison was changed.
